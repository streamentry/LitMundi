# Governance and continuity

## Principles and present stewardship

Textual integrity, public access, attribution, literary fidelity, lawful reuse and portability guide the project. LitMundi uses AI-first publication under its versioned quality policy, with optional human review and reader corrections after release.

The GitHub repository owner, **streamentry**, is the sole known steward. No external institution, mirror custodian, successor or reviewer is represented as having accepted a role. A future handoff should name responsibilities and obtain each person's actual consent in a reviewable record.

## Decisions and publication

Architectural and publication-policy decisions belong in [ADRs](docs/adr/README.md); literary choices, alternatives and disagreements belong with the edition. Corrections use reviewable changes and preserve stable identifiers. Source provenance and earlier assessments remain available.

Under [rubric v2](docs/QUALITY.md), a translation needs one complete AI assessment with an **unrounded weighted score of at least 8.6**, exact final source/target hashes, every canonical passage covered and no unresolved current major or critical issue. The weights remain 35/25/15/15/10, with half-point category scores. V2 imposes no additional category floors or mandatory second assessment.

Publication also requires source-rights clearance, documented lawful reuse, complete text/alignment and valid publication metadata. It does **not** require a human reviewer, human editor or human editorial attestation. AI assessments are disclosed as evaluative model judgments, not human certification. CI verifies structured evidence and integrity; it cannot guarantee literary merit or legal validity.

[ADR-0009](docs/adr/0009-ai-first-publication.md) supersedes the earlier human-review prerequisites. V1, earlier reviews and dated reports remain unchanged as historical evidence. They do not become v2 approvals simply because the policy has changed.

## Feedback, corrections and accountability

Human readings, literary reviews and reader reports are welcome after release. Record actual identities, roles and contributions accurately; never invent a reviewer, permission, endorsement or institutional relationship. A voluntary human review may strengthen an edition without becoming a prerequisite for publication.

Investigate reported errors with the exact source and passage IDs. Preserve disagreements and resolution history. A confirmed current major or critical issue blocks the affected edition until resolved. Material corrections require re-evaluation and a new publication decision; any changed source or target needs current matching assessment evidence before republication.

The maintainer remains responsible for repository decisions, correcting known problems and representing the evidence honestly. Automated checks and AI scores do not remove that responsibility.

## Rights and disputes

The MIT software/documentation license does not license the literary corpus. Each edition retains its own source-rights assessment, attribution and reuse terms.

AI-only output defaults to CC BY 4.0 (`CC-BY-4.0`) **only to the extent the publisher controls applicable rights**, following [AI content policy](docs/AI-CONTENT-POLICY.md). The declaration preserves any such rights while allowing open reuse under the stated terms. It does not create copyright, imply human authorship, impose new restrictions on public-domain material or license third-party source rights. Do not invent human contributions or incorporate actual protected human contributions without a lawful basis.

Report textual or rights concerns through the repository's issue channel; use [Security](SECURITY.md) for private security disclosures. For a credible rights dispute, withhold the disputed edition while investigating. Preserve provenance, the concern and resolution history to the extent lawful. An AI legal assessment is not conclusive authority.

## If the original maintainer becomes unavailable

Another organization can continue the work without the original domain or GitHub account:

1. Obtain a lawful repository copy or editorial archive and an independently retained digest. Follow the [offline restoration procedure](docs/PRESERVATION.md), documenting the result and provenance.
2. Inspect the per-edition rights manifest. Reuse software/docs under MIT and literary content only under its actual rights or license. Keep uncleared material separate until its reuse basis is established.
3. Preserve work, edition and passage IDs, source citations, attribution, licensing and review history. A new translation or different source witness receives a new edition ID.
4. Recheck current publication evidence and applicable jurisdictional rights. A valid checksum proves identity, not the truth of a rights claim or literary assessment. Document the successor's decisions and accurately retain AI versus human attribution.
5. Publish on a suitable static host, clearly identify the successor or independent fork, and record custody. The old account, domain and Git history are not needed to read the exported corpus.

This is a lawful technical continuation path, not an automatic transfer of trademarks, domains, accounts or private rights. The project has no signed succession agreement or claimed external mirror. GitHub's 30-day CI artifact retention does not establish independent custody.

## Sustainable scope

Publish eligible editions, use optional reader feedback to improve them, and record actual editorial effort when planning further work. Record real custodians and restoration dates when they exist. If funding is collected later, publish its conditions and basic accounts; no funding body or council is implied by this policy.
