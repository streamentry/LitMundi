# AI-produced literary content: provenance and reuse

Policy adopted **1 November 2026** alongside [ADR-0009](adr/0009-ai-first-publication.md), following the project owner's instruction to publish eligible AI-reviewed work and accept human feedback afterward. This policy removes a mandatory human editor/reviewer from the release process. It does not treat an AI score as copyright clearance.

## Publication and rights are separate decisions

An edition may pass the literary gate with one complete, current v2 AI assessment and a weighted score of at least 8.6. Its source, text coverage, rights and evidence must also be in order. No human translator, editor, reviewer or signature is invented to fill a metadata field.

The original source remains identified by its own edition, witness and jurisdiction-specific rights record. A public-domain original does not clear a modern translation, introduction or facsimile for reuse. The current AI translations were generated and revised directly against their cited historical originals; no modern intermediary translation was used as their source.

## Open reuse of project output

For each AI-produced translation whose edition metadata selects `CC-BY-4.0`, LitMundi's publishing maintainer offers **any copyright and related rights they control** in that edition under [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en). This is a scoped open license, not an assertion that every AI-produced passage attracts copyright. Rights that do not exist are not created by a license. Public-domain material acquires no additional restriction through this statement; third-party rights remain separate.

Use the edition-specific metadata as the authority. Retain the original author and witness, the LitMundi edition identifier, AI translation/review disclosure, the license reference and an indication of your changes where the license applies. Even where attribution is not legally required, preserving that provenance helps readers trace errors and compare editions. The original author is credited for the original work, not falsely credited as author of the Vietnamese wording.

The current AI-only records identify the actual tool use and explicitly record no human creative contributors. They do not claim exclusive human authorship, a human editorial endorsement, ownership of the original source, or worldwide copyright protection. If a future human supplies copyrightable creative changes, record that contributor and their actual reuse permission; an AI review cannot grant rights on their behalf. Human feedback itself remains welcome without becoming a pre-publication review requirement.

## Basis and limits

- [OpenAI Services Agreement, section 4](https://openai.com/policies/services-agreement/), effective 1 January 2026, assigns OpenAI's interests in output to its customer to the extent permitted by law. It also places responsibility for inputs and use of outputs on the customer and does not promise unique output. This contractual allocation does not establish copyrightability or clear someone else's rights.
- [CC BY 4.0 legal code, sections 1–3](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en) scopes licensed rights to rights the licensor can grant and provides the terms for sharing, adaptation and attribution. It does not manufacture rights in unprotected material.
- The [U.S. Copyright Office's January 2025 copyrightability report announcement](https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html) distinguishes sufficient human expressive authorship from machine-determined expression. That U.S. position is not a conclusion about every jurisdiction or every future contribution.

These references were checked on 1 November 2026. The project therefore records assessed source rights and a scoped reuse license, rather than inventing human ownership or declaring all AI output public domain everywhere. A credible rights dispute sets `rights.status` to `disputed` and blocks a new release until resolved. A serious text correction likewise requires revised content, fresh hashes and a new qualifying assessment.
