# Rights, licensing, and provenance
**Not legal advice. Jurisdiction and specific editions matter.**

The MIT license applies to the software and documentation; it does **not** grant rights in literary works, scans, images, editions, or translations. Every real edition needs metadata recording: exact physical/digital source and bibliographic citation; original author/date/death date; edition editor and publication date; provenance URL and capture date; rights rationale by jurisdiction and intended distribution; translator/editor credits and permissions; translation license with SPDX-like identifier or exact terms; AI involvement disclosure; reviewer signoff; source content checksums.

Public-domain status of an original does not waive copyright in a modern translation, introduction, annotated edition or images. Non-commercial permissions generally conflict with ads. Do not assume AI-only output is copyrightable, or that copyright-free texts have no moral-rights considerations. Takedown requests should temporarily unpublish disputed material and preserve an internal audit trail until reviewed.

**Recommended policy**: original texts from explicitly validated public-domain editions; new human-reviewed translations released under CC BY 4.0 or CC0 only with explicit informed contributor consent. The project must not assert a blanket CC0 corpus license. No unsigned publication.
