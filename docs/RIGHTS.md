# Rights, licensing, and provenance
**Not legal advice. Jurisdiction and specific editions matter.**

The MIT license applies to the software and documentation; it does **not** grant rights in literary works, scans, images, editions, or translations. Every real edition needs metadata recording: exact physical/digital source and bibliographic citation; original author/date/death date; edition editor and publication date; provenance URL and capture date; rights rationale by jurisdiction and intended distribution; actual contributor credits and permissions; translation license with SPDX-like identifier or exact terms; AI involvement and assessment evidence; source and target content checksums. A human reviewer or signature is not required for an AI-only edition under [ADR-0009](adr/0009-ai-first-publication.md).

Public-domain status of an original does not waive copyright in a modern translation, introduction, annotated edition or images. Non-commercial permissions generally conflict with ads. Do not assume AI-only output is copyrightable, or that copyright-free texts have no moral-rights considerations. Takedown requests should temporarily unpublish disputed material and preserve an internal audit trail until reviewed.

**Current policy:** original texts retain their assessed, edition-specific rights evidence. AI-only translations default to CC BY 4.0 only for rights the publishing maintainer controls, under the scoped [AI content policy](AI-CONTENT-POLICY.md). This does not create copyright, imply human authorship or restrict public-domain material. An actual human creative contribution requires that contributor's documented reuse permission. Do not invent a contributor or consent record. The project must not assert a blanket CC0 corpus license; literary assessment and rights clearance remain separate checks.

## Pilot rights record

The 2026-10-31 Oscar Wilde pilot recorded an assessed public-domain source, its exact 1913 Methuen witness, digitization credit and supporting documents in `corpus/catalog/editions.json`. At that time the Vietnamese draft lacked a cleared publication license. That historical state remains documented in the dated records.

On 1 November 2026, the project adopted the scoped AI-content reuse policy and v2 publication rubric. The current catalog records the rights basis, original sources and AI provenance of all three complete Vietnamese translations. Wilde and Machado meet the current literary gate and are published; Pardo Bazán remains a draft because its weighted score is 8.55. CI requires current source/target hashes, complete coverage, rights evidence and a qualifying AI assessment; no human clearance is a publication prerequisite.
