# SEO and generative search research — 1 November 2026

## Decision and scope

Prioritize the complete reading editions: make each work easy to identify, read,
compare with its source and cite. LitMundi's opportunity is useful Vietnamese
literary access with transparent provenance. Technical eligibility supports that
goal; it cannot establish that the site is the best translation, the top search
result or a preferred source for an AI answer.

This document records public research checked on **2026-11-01**, the implementation
in this change and its pre-release verification. Final revision, CI and deployment
results belong in the implementation pull request. Local checks are distinguished
from authenticated search-engine observations throughout.

The implementation preserves the existing AI-first publication threshold of
**8.6 or higher** and the existing rights, completeness and assessment checks. It
does not introduce a mandatory human approval step. The 500-work Vietnamese
backlog is an editorial plan, not a justification for publishing hundreds of
thin, unfinished or keyword-variant pages.

See [SEO.md](SEO.md) for the sitemap inventory, canonical edition policy,
language relationships, truthful modification dates and GitHub Pages robots.txt
boundary. Those remain the technical foundation.

## Evidence and its limits

- Research uses official Google, Microsoft/Bing, IndexNow and OpenAI guidance.
  Public literary publications are used only to observe existing title variants
  and attribution, not as technical SEO authorities.
- The Semrush API returned **insufficient units**. No keyword volumes,
  difficulty scores, traffic forecasts or competitor traffic estimates were
  obtained. None are inferred from the public search samples.
- The previous local `dist` output was stale and is not a valid performance
  baseline. This work makes no before/after speed, traffic, ranking or citation
  improvement claim.
- No authenticated Google Search Console or Bing Webmaster Tools data was
  available to this research. Public result samples cannot establish complete
  indexing coverage. Google explicitly states that
  [`site:` results are not exhaustive](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/search-operators/all-search-site).
- Research observations and implementation suggestions are separate. A feature
  recommended here is not evidence that it has shipped or improved results.

## What the current official guidance means for LitMundi

### Useful editions and honest provenance come first

Google's [AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide),
introduced in May 2026, emphasizes distinctive value for the reader and ordinary
search foundations. Applied to LitMundi, a defensible direction is complete text,
source comparison, reliable bibliographic information, stable passage links and
clear edition attribution. This is an implementation judgment, not a measured
ranking factor for this site.

Google's [people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
asks publishers to make authorship, creation process and purpose clear. Readers
should be able to distinguish the original literary author, LitMundi's AI
translation, its assessment process and any actual human contribution. Do not
invent translators, credentials, endorsements or editorial oversight.

The [AI-content guidance updated on 1 October 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)
explicitly recommends manual factchecking and review, including generated titles,
descriptions and structured data. AI-only assessment does not demonstrate that
human review occurred; that remains a quality-assurance gap. This research does
not silently change the site's publication gate or label an AI-reviewed edition
as human-reviewed. New editorial summaries and author facts should be checked
against the actual text and cited sources, with uncertainty left visible.

Google's [scaled-content abuse policy](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
concerns large amounts of low-value material produced primarily to manipulate
ranking, regardless of how it was created. Do not turn the 500-work plan into
indexable placeholders, lightly varied summaries or one page per possible query.

### Titles, descriptions and language must describe the actual page

[Google's title guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link)
supports descriptive, concise titles that identify the main content in its
language. Use the edition title and author, with language or edition context when
useful. For example, a Vietnamese reader title can identify the translated title,
Oscar Wilde and the Vietnamese edition without repeating a string of keywords.
Do not rename an approved translation merely to imitate another publisher's title.

[Snippets](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet) are
primarily derived from visible page content and may vary by query. A unique,
accurate description sets expectations; it does not control the final snippet.
Summaries should be useful to a reader and avoid revealing a story's ending
unless clearly presented as analysis with spoilers.

[Google's multilingual guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites)
uses visible text to determine language and warns about changing only interface
boilerplate around identical main content. Keep interface locale and literary
language distinct. Preserve one canonical reader per edition, real translation
alternates, and optional source comparison. Do not advertise a French interface
around an English original as a French literary translation.

### Structured data describes reality; it is not a quality certificate

Follow [Google's structured-data policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies):
markup should describe relevant visible content and use accurate entities and
relationships. Work, edition, author, source and breadcrumb information can make
the page easier to interpret. Distinguish a work's original publication date from
the publication date of LitMundi's edition; omit unknown dates rather than infer
them from a build.

An internal AI rubric score is not a reader rating, an `aggregateRating`, or an
independent review. Do not invent ISBNs or attach irrelevant schema types. Valid
markup alone does not guarantee a rich result or AI citation.

The [Google documentation changelog](https://developers.google.com/search/updates)
records that FAQ rich results stopped appearing on **7 May 2026**. A visible FAQ
can still answer real reader questions, but `FAQPage` is not a current Google
rich-result tactic. The [sitelinks search box was also retired](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/10/sitelinks-search-box);
do not add `SearchAction` solely to obtain that feature.

### Discovery and AI access

Use ordinary [crawlable links with descriptive anchor text](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable).
Author and language browsing should lead to actual published works. Keep full
reading content available in HTML, retain mobile usability and avoid making
discovery depend on a JavaScript-only search control.

The Google AI guide explicitly says `llms.txt` neither helps nor harms Google
Search visibility or rankings. It requires no special AI markup, prescribed
writing style or tiny content chunks. Preserve literary paragraph structure;
add useful headings around contextual material instead of rewriting the story
for a presumed AI parser.

[OpenAI's crawler documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots)
distinguishes `OAI-SearchBot` for ChatGPT search from `GPTBot` for potential model
training. These permissions are independent. `ChatGPT-User` represents
user-triggered visits and is not the control for automatic search inclusion.
Allowing the search crawler supports access; it does not guarantee selection.
The documentation does not prescribe an `llms.txt` ranking mechanism.

The effective robots file is at the host root, not `/LitMundi/robots.txt`.
On 2026-11-01 the root file returned 404 and the project file returned 200. A
missing root robots file does not block crawling, but it supplies no sitemap
declaration. Do not claim that a project-level file controls the host's crawlers.

## Implementation in this change

| Area | Scope and rationale | Constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Localized metadata and summaries | Specific titles/descriptions, original-title and author context, and concise useful work summaries for the supported interfaces. | Describe the actual edition; preserve approved literary text/title and avoid unsupported facts or keyword stuffing. |
| Author directory and About pages | Useful browsing and transparent explanation of the project in English, Vietnamese, French and Spanish. | Include actual published works and verified identity/process information; do not generate empty profiles for the backlog. |
| Citation details | Make the edition, original author, source, language and canonical link easy to identify and reuse. | Separate work-level facts from edition-level facts; do not invent dates, translators or bibliographic identifiers. |
| JSON-LD and breadcrumbs | Faithful work/edition/author relationships and navigation matching the visible page. | No fake ratings, special AI schema claims or unsupported rich-result promises. |
| Social cards | Consistent, legible previews that identify the work and author when links are shared. | This improves presentation; it is not evidence of ranking gains. Use accurate labels and assets with known rights. |
| Regression checks | Verify metadata, public routes, language/canonical relationships, structured data, social assets and the existing publication exclusions. | Test meaningful output and reader behavior. Passing tests proves the tested behavior, not search-engine acceptance. |

The implementation adds twenty localized work summaries, eight author-directory
and About pages, edition citation details, shared site identities and breadcrumbs,
six social cards and a logo. It preserves the ten published editions and their
literary text, titles, stable identifiers and assessment evidence. The Google
verification and async Analytics tags added upstream are retained. Reading-position
copy now describes browser-local storage without making a blanket no-tracking claim.

## Current measurement tools and hosting limits

### Google Search Console

The [Search generative AI control](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16908024)
and [Generative AI performance report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139)
were globally rolled out by **31 August 2026**. The control defaults to inclusion;
a child URL-prefix property can inherit its parent setting or override it. Check
the setting with owner access, rather than inventing a required opt-in file.

The AI report provides **impressions**, grouped by pages, countries, dates and
devices, for supported Search AI features. A report may not appear when there are
too few impressions. It excludes Search Labs experiments, uses different
property/page aggregation, and generally attributes page data to canonical URLs.
The report documentation describes an impression report, not a per-answer
citation-position or AI-click report. Keep its metrics distinct from ordinary
Search performance clicks and impressions; do not add overlapping totals as if
they represented separate audiences.

### Bing Webmaster Tools and IndexNow

[Bing AI Performance](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/2026/2/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview/)
launched in public preview on 10 February 2026. It reports total citations,
average cited pages, page-level citation activity and sampled grounding queries
across supported Microsoft and partner surfaces. These are not rankings,
authority scores or positions within a particular answer. Aggregated coverage and
sampled queries cannot be treated as a complete log of all AI use.

[Microsoft's content guidance](https://about.ads.microsoft.com/en/blog/post/october-2025/optimizing-your-content-for-inclusion-in-ai-search-answers)
supports clear headings, contextual titles and evidence for factual claims. For
LitMundi, apply this to navigation and editorial context while preserving the
literary text. There is no guaranteed selection formula.

[IndexNow](https://www.indexnow.org/documentation) is an optional later discovery
improvement, not a completed part of this release. A key file under `/LitMundi/`
can be supplied through `keyLocation`, scoping notifications to that path without
requiring control of the origin root. Notify participating engines of real added,
changed or deleted canonical URLs after deployment. A 200 response acknowledges
receipt; it does not prove indexing. The [FAQ](https://www.indexnow.org/faq)
recommends meaningful changes rather than repeated notifications for unchanged
pages. Do not describe IndexNow as Google submission or a ranking boost.

### GitHub Pages branding

[Google's site-name feature](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/site-names)
supports domains and subdomains, not subdirectory sites. `WebSite` markup under
`/LitMundi/` cannot guarantee an independent LitMundi site name. A custom domain
would provide a clearer branding and origin-control boundary, but migration is
a separate decision and not an automatic ranking improvement.

## Owner measurement plan

The time windows below start after the verified deployment. They are observation
milestones, not deadlines by which engines must index or rank the site. Owner
access is required; unavailable data should be recorded as unavailable, not zero.

### First 7 days

1. **Record the release.** Save the deployed commit, deployment time, intended
   canonical inventory and actual verification results. Treat this as the start
   of the observation period; do not compare it to the stale `dist` output.
2. **Verify the exact property.** Use the Google Search Console URL-prefix
   property `https://streamentry.github.io/LitMundi/` and the corresponding Bing
   site. Submit `https://streamentry.github.io/LitMundi/sitemap-index.xml` once
   through each verified account. Record submission time and processing status
   separately from indexing status.
3. **Inspect representative URLs.** Check the published Vietnamese canonical
   readers, representative source readers, and the new directory/About routes.
   Record response, robots eligibility, declared and selected canonical where
   available, and any indexing reason. If a representative noncanonical wrapper
   is excluded as a duplicate with the correct canonical, that is expected.
4. **Check AI participation settings.** Inspect Google's Search generative AI
   control and inherited setting. Check whether Google and Bing AI reports are
   available. Record missing reports and their possible thresholds without
   labeling them a technical failure.
5. **Export the first available observations.** Keep ordinary Search queries,
   pages, countries/devices, impressions and clicks separate from Google AI
   impressions and Bing AI citations. Record each export's date range, timezone,
   filters and property. Distinguish branded/title queries from broader reading
   queries without claiming a volume estimate.
6. **Resolve actual defects.** Fix demonstrated broken links, wrong language,
   unavailable canonical targets, metadata mistakes or processing errors. Do not
   churn titles, dates or publication policy because a new page has no impressions.

### By day 30

1. **Compare like with like.** If enough data exists, compare equal-length
   post-release windows with the same filters. Record changes in indexed
   canonical coverage, observed queries, landing pages, impressions and clicks.
   New content, indexing delays and small samples prevent attributing every
   change to this release.
2. **Review reader intent.** Use actual query/page pairs to identify confusion
   about title, author, language or full-text availability. Improve a specific
   existing page when evidence supports it; do not create a new page for each
   spelling or query variant.
3. **Review AI observations separately.** Report Google AI impressions and Bing
   citations using each platform's definitions. Inspect which pages appear and
   any sampled Bing grounding queries. Do not combine these into a fabricated
   cross-platform ranking or a single comparable citation total.
4. **Check substantive usefulness.** Review any actual reader corrections and
   voluntary feedback about translation, citation and mobile reading. Document
   discovered errors and fixes. Do not invent engagement or satisfaction metrics,
   install unsolicited tracking, or equate an AI rubric score with reader approval.
5. **Choose the next small investment.** Prioritize recurring factual errors,
   confusing edition identity, useful missing collections or source information
   before expanding the backlog. Consider IndexNow or a custom domain only for
   their concrete operational benefits. If evidence is insufficient, continue
   observing and improve confirmed issues without a growth claim.
